# Device parity — MotionLab against Fender Studio Pro 8

Device-by-device gap list for **faces**: what each of our devices draws, what the reference's
counterpart draws, and what is missing. It is the operational companion to
`docs/REFERENCE-FSP8.md` §§13–14, which is where the evidence and the confidence markers
live; nothing is asserted here that is not sourced there.

Scope note: this file is about **device faces** — controls, meters, visualisations. Rack
behaviour, the plug-in header, presets and console furniture are `REFERENCE-FSP8.md` §9 and
are not repeated.

Read against `src/model/effects.ts` (the `EFFECTS` array and `WAM_SPEC`),
`src/components/mixer/PluginFace.tsx` (`faceKindOf`, `EffectVisual` and the face
components), `src/components/synth/SynthPanel.tsx`, `src/components/sampler/SamplerPanel.tsx`.

Our catalogue is **34 effect kinds including a third-party `wam` placeholder**, and **three
instruments** (MotionSynth, the sampler in its four views, and the classic TX Drum Kit).

## 3. Top gaps, in priority order

Ordered by what a user notices first, weighted by how cheap the fix is.

1. **The Tuner draws an oscilloscope instead of a tuner.** No note name, no cents, no
   flat/sharp arrows, no strobe. The device does not do the one thing it is named for, and
   its calibration range already matches the reference exactly.
2. **Per-band gain reduction on the Multiband**, drawn the reference's way — input level along
   the top of each band and output along the bottom, reddening under attenuation. One shared
   GR bar for three bands hides which band is working, which is the only question a multiband
   raises.
3. **A live spectrum behind the EQ curve**, with the reference's mode selector including
   **None** and its **6 / 12 / 24 dB level-range** switch. Every other EQ a user has met has
   this, and we already own an analyser tap.
4. **The GR line on the dynamics graph, and a maximum-reduction hold on the readout.** The
   graph currently shows the law but not the behaviour; the reference draws the live reduction
   descending from the 0 dB point and holds the peak figure.
5. **Sidechain and key-filter controls across the dynamics family**, plus the compressor's
   behaviour group (Auto, Auto Gain, Look Ahead). Currently no dynamics device in our set can
   be keyed from anything, which rules out a large fraction of real mixing work.
6. **Audition buttons.** De-Esser Listen and Solo, EQ per-band Solo, and a Ping-style
   audition on the reverb. Roughly one reference device in three has a button whose only job
   is to let you hear what the device is keying on; we have none.
7. **Input/output metering on every device that changes level** — starting with `trim`, which
   draws nothing at all, then the limiter's output meter with its K-System scale switch and
   clip counter, then the Tricomp pattern of a meter beside each gain knob.
8. **Amp Sim needs a pedalboard and a microphone section.** Eight ordered slots with a
   draggable pre-amp/effects-loop divider, and three mics with blend, polarity and distance.
   It is the reference's only device that draws its own signal chain, and ours is a curve and
   a dropdown.
9. **Animate the modulation faces.** Six devices share one static LFO drawing. Making the
   trace move in time, and adding the moving notch response for the flanger and phaser and a
   visible speed ramp for the rotary, converts a picture of a setting into a picture of a
   process.
10. **Early reflections and a self-adjusting time axis on the reverb display**, plus Room
    Reverb's geometry group with its **W×D×H readout in real units**. A reverb display that
    cannot show the ER/LR balance cannot be used to set it.

Beyond ten, in rough order: a **Groove Delay-style tap grid with a parameter selector**; a
**Phase Meter** and a **Level Meter** as devices; **per-pad outputs and banks in the drum
view**; **display settings on every face** (the reference's faces almost all have some);
**Fat Channel XT's Stacked Mode** as a pattern for any multi-processor device; and a **Mix
control on the dynamics devices** for parallel compression.

## 4. Where we are ahead

Worth recording so "parity" does not become a one-way ratchet:

- **`gainMatch`** has no counterpart in the reference.
- **The sampler's zone map is key _and_ velocity**, where the reference's Multi Instrument
  maps keys only and requires a separate Note FX for velocity splits.
- **Our sample-window handles take the arrow keys**; nothing documented for the reference's
  samplers does.
- **The TX Drum Kit's strike-position velocity** — where you hit the pad sets how hard —
  has no equivalent on a reference pad.
- **Our shaper faces draw the actual curve the audio is filled from**, rather than a picture
  assembled from the parameter values; and the dynamics faces draw the law by asking for it
  by name, so the knee drawn is the knee heard. No source describes the reference doing
  either.
- **Delay and reverb displays.** The reference's Analog Delay and Beat Delay have no
  confirmed graphics at all, and Mixverb's display is unknown; our tap diagram and decay
  envelope are additions, not catch-up.
